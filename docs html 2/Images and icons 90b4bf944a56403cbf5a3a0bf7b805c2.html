Informative vs decorative images and icons

Some images and icons appear as a way to decorate the page, such as the icons on the Your pension cards. They do not help users understand the information being conveyed, and have no function. They only help to make the page look more attractive.

If images have no informative value and aren’t links or buttons, they will be decorative images and do not need captions.

UX should identify informative and decorative images and supply captions where needed when handing over deliverables.

Writing alt text

Prioritise important information in alt text

Put the most important information at the beginning. This makes it faster for users to get the information they need.

Keep alt text short, unless it is a complex image

Alt text should be as short as possible, nothing more than a phrase or short sentence. If you need more, then it will be a complex image, and should be explained in more detail within the page with a short alternative text description.

Give the same information as the image

We should convey to users the same information in the alt text as if they could see the image. This is the same for images that convey an action, such as an image link, button, or image map area. It should clearly identify the link destination or button.

Unnecessary words

Words like “image”, “icon”, or “picture” don’t need to be included in the alt text. People who can see will know this already, and screen readers announce the presence of an image. In some situations, it may be important to distinguish between photographs, or illustrations and so on.

Plain text vs embedded text

Use plain text where possible instead of text embedded in images. Logos and decorative images can use embedded text.
