Alert

Figma linkStorybook link

Alerts communicate important information at the top of the page. Alerts are persistent but can disappear when the user takes action or navigates away from the page.

We use alerts when the message is relevant to the entire page rather than just a specific section.

Use when

  • Communicating updates or changes to system status.
  • Communicating information that applies to an entire page.
  • Communicating connectivity or authentication issues.

Don't use when

  • Validating errors in forms, use Error list instead.
  • Information only applies to a section of the page, use Inline alert instead.

Variants

There are four variants of an alert, depending on the previous or required action:

VariantPurpose
SuccessLets the user know they've successfully completed a task.
ErrorAn error has stopped a user from completing a task, and they have to take action to continue.
WarningShown when some information will affect users and they may need to take action. It won't block them from continuing.
InformationProvides information that users might want to know, but won't block them from continuing.

Elements

  1. Icon – Informs users of the alert variant at a glance.
  1. Title (optional) – Gives a quick overview of the alert. Usually the most actionable details.
  1. Body text (optional) – Provides additional detail.
  1. Links (optional) – Links to next steps when relevant.

Visual

Placement

Always position at the top of the page, below the page heading but above all other content. All content below the alert should be relevant to the alert message.

Size

Always full-width.

Groups

Use up to two alerts on the same page. Any more than this may be overwhelming for the user.

Colour

Alert border and icons use specific base-2 colour palettes. See more details on Colours.

VariantColour token
Successpositive-primary core
Errornegative-primary core
Warningwarning-primary core

Behaviour

Alerts are persistent and cannot be dismissed.

The height of the alert expands downwards to accommodate more content.

Content

Key elements

Title

  • Should be short and descriptive, explaining the most important information.
  • If possible, communicate the main message of the alert with just the title.
  • Use a full stop if the title is a full sentence.

Body

  • Give additional context around the action only if needed. You can include links that communicate next steps.
  • Keep to one or two short sentences.

Patterns

Success

PurposeCommunicate successful change of state
Patterns1. <You've verbed the noun>
2. <You've verbed the noun. When this will happen.>
3. <Your the noun is change.>
Example1. You’ve saved your changes.
2. You’ve saved your changes. Your changes will take effect from the next payroll cycle.
3. Your retirement age is now 55.

Error

Check our guidance on using the right tone when writing error messages.

User error

PurposeSpecific error caused by a user-initiated action. We should have details of why it happened.
Pattern<What we could not do. What was wrong. What to do to fix it.>
ExampleWe couldn’t process your file. The file format is not supported. Please use the CSV template to ensure your file is in the correct format.

System error

PurposeError caused by a system failure, such as a 404. Generally, we don't have the details available to surface on the front end so have to be generic.
Pattern<Sorry, we couldn't verb the noun. Please try again later.>
ExampleSorry, we couldn't update your password. Please try again later.

Warning

PurposeWarn users that an action is blocked and why.
Pattern1. <You can't verb your the noun. Reason why and when this may end.>
2. <Your the noun are verb. Reason why and when this may end.>
Example1. <You can’t change your investments right now. We’ve temporarily disabled this feature while we make some changes to your account.>
2. <Your pension contributions are paused. Contributions are not currently being made into your pension. They will automatically restart 3 months after you paused them. >

Information

Information alerts don't follow a typical pattern. Generally, be direct and keep the detail relevant to the journey and current page.
