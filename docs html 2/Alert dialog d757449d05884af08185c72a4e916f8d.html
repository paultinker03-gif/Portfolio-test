Alert dialog

Figma linkStorybook link

Alert dialogs are modals that give users the opportunity to confirm or cancel an action. They interrupt a flow and require action to disappear. We use alert dialogs to confirm destructive and positive actions, such as deleting data or opting into a scheme. Often, this action can't be reversed once it's done.

Use when

  • confirming a final action.
  • interrupting the user.

Examples

Elements

  1. Title – Provides context about the action.
  1. Description – Describes the consequences of the action.
  1. Text input (optional) – An extra point of friction that requires users to enter a specific line of text before continuing.
  1. Negative or positive primary button – Allows users to continue with the destructive or positive action.
  1. Secondary button – Gives an option to dismiss the alert and cancel the destructive or positive action.
  1. Dismissible icon – Another option to dismiss and cancel the action.

Variants

There are two variants of an alert dialog, depending on the required action:

VariantPurpose
PositiveAsking the user to confirm a positive action e.g. are you sure you want to opt in to your pension scheme?
DestructiveAsking the user to confirm a destructive action e.g are you sure you want to opt out?

Visual

Devices

On mobile, we use a Drawer so the modal slides in from the bottom and covers the entire screen.

On larger viewports, we use a typical modal that overlays all content and only covers a centre portion of the screen.

Alignment

Left-align all text elements including the text input.

Right-align the buttons on desktop.

Behaviour

Interaction

Users can dismiss the alert by:

  • selecting the dismissible icon in top right
  • selecting the secondary button

Content

The heading and body copy usually follows the same pattern.

Heading

A question to confirm the action – "Are you absolutely sure?"

Body copy

Usually "Please type the following text below to confirm: I wish to {action}". For example, I wish to opt out.

Buttons

The negative button text should match the action used in the body copy. In the example above, the button should be "Opt out".
