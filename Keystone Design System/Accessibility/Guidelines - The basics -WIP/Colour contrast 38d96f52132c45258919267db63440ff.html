Colour contrast

Colour contrast is a critical aspect of website accessibility, ensuring that content is readable and usable by people with various visual impairments.

To improve colour contrast:

Use Sufficient Contrast Ratios:
Text has a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1. Large text can be a 3 to 1 ratio if it’s over 24px, or bold and over 19px. Usable components like form fields and buttons, and graphics like pie charts and line graphs should have a contrast ratio of 3 to 1.

Test Contrast Ratios:
Use online tools or browser extensions to check the contrast ratios of your colour combinations. Ensure that text and background colours meet the WCAG requirements.

Avoid Colour as the Sole Indicator:
Don't rely solely on colour to convey information. Use additional cues like text labels, patterns, or icons.

Consider Different Types of Colour Blindness:
Be mindful of how colour choices may affect individuals with various types of colour blindness. Simulators can help you assess this.

Borders

Borders serve various purposes, such as indicating boundaries, separating content, or highlighting elements. To ensure accessibility:

Contrast with Background:
The border colour should have sufficient contrast with the background colour. It should meet the minimum contrast ratios defined by WCAG (e.g., 3:1 for larger borders, 4.5:1 for smaller borders).

Focus Indication:
Borders are often used to indicate focus on interactive elements like buttons or form fields. Ensure that the focus border is distinct and highly visible, meeting the necessary contrast requirements.

Error and Validation Borders:
When using borders to indicate errors or validation messages, ensure that the border colour clearly communicates the status (e.g., red for errors). Again, meet contrast standards for readability.

Border Thickness:
Consider the thickness of borders. Thicker borders can be easier to perceive, but ensure they don't overwhelm the content.

Background Colour Variations:
If the design includes multiple background colours, adjust border colours accordingly to maintain contrast consistency.

Icons

Colour contrast is crucial when using icons on a website or interface, especially if those icons convey important information or actions. To ensure accessibility with icons:

Icon Design:
Use clear and easily recognisable icons. Simple, well-defined shapes are more accessible than intricate designs.

Colour Choices:
Ensure that the colours used in icons provide sufficient contrast with the background or surrounding elements.

Avoid Sole Reliance on Colour:
Icons should not rely solely on colour to convey meaning. Use additional visual cues such as labels, text, or symbols to reinforce the message.

Use Patterns or Textures:
Incorporate patterns or textures within icons to enhance visibility for users with colour vision deficiencies.

Responsive Icons:
Icons should adapt to different screen sizes and resolutions while maintaining clarity and contrast.

Text

Colour contrast is crucial for ensuring that text on a website or interface is readable and accessible to all users. Here are some key considerations:

Text Style:
Choose clear and legible fonts. Avoid decorative fonts that may be difficult to read, especially when combined with low contrast.

Avoid Red-Green Combinations:
Refrain from using red-green colour combinations, as these can be problematic for individuals with colour vision deficiencies.

Link Contrast:
Ensure that links have distinct colours and underline them, or use another visual cue, to indicate their clickable nature.

Text on images and videos

Ensuring proper colour contrast for text on images is crucial for accessibility and readability. When text is overlaid on images, use semi-transparent backgrounds or contrasting text colours to maintain readability.

When placing text on images, consider the following:

Background Contrast:
Ensure that the text has sufficient contrast with the image background to make it easily readable. Use colour combinations that meet WCAG contrast guidelines.

Text Placement:
Position the text on areas of the image where there's a clear contrast between the text and the background. Avoid placing text over complex or busy portions of the image.

Text Colour:
Choose text colours that stand out from the image background. Use dark text on light backgrounds or vice versa, depending on the image content.

Semi-Transparent Overlays:
If the image has varying backgrounds, consider adding a semi-transparent overlay behind the text to improve contrast.

Font Size and Style:
Use legible fonts and an appropriate font size. Ensure the text remains clear and readable, especially when scaling down the image for different screen sizes.

Text Shadow or Outline:
Adding a subtle shadow or outline to the text can help it stand out against the image background.
